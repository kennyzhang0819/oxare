# Animals

The soft look (`ENV.props` `soft`, docs/colors.md "Looks") draws the obstacles as simplified animals. This is the design brief every animal follows, what each one is and why, and what is still to come. Code: `src/scene.ts` (the bodies), `src/rails.ts` (the snake), `src/level.ts` (the parts the physics must know about), `src/palette.ts` `ANIMALS` (their colours).

## The brief

- **One animal per piece type, picked for the piece's silhouette.** The animal has to be the shape the piece already is, so the player reads the obstacle first and the animal second: a blocky piece is a blocky animal, a thin wide one is flat-sided, a tall round one is tall. Never bend the piece to fit the animal.
- **Two or three iconic features, nothing else.** Each animal is its plain body plus the two or three things that make it that animal (ears, fins, horns, bands). No fur, no texture, no limbs that do nothing. If a feature is not needed to name the animal, leave it off.
- **Primitives only.** Bodies are rounded boxes, cylinders and squashed balls; details are more balls, thin boxes and short tubes. No image textures on an animal. Cel shading and the ink outline do the rest, so a shape that reads in flat colour is the goal.
- **Features big enough to read at ball scale.** The ball is 1 unit across and the camera sits behind it; a feature under 0.1 vanishes. Ears, fins and horns are 0.3 and up; eyes 0.08 to 0.1.
- **Eyes are dark ellipsoids, no outline of their own, no whites.** Every animal wears a pair, slightly flattened against the surface they sit on. They are the one thing all animals share, so they are drawn one way (`eye` in scene.ts): the ink colour `ANIMALS.eye`, a hair taller than wide, poking 0.03 out of the body.
- **Faces on the side the ball meets.** The face goes on local +z (the way `rot` 0 faces), or on both long sides for a flat animal the ball can meet from either side (the fish). A long animal faces away from its own body (the snake).
- **Flat details lie on the surface like paint.** Noses, teeth, inner ears, spots and whiskers sink into the body until they stand at most `PAINT` (0.005) proud, like every other prop's panels and strips, so they never need a collider. They carry no ink outline of their own (`blob` drops it for anything flattened to a decal): sunk into a body, a decal's outline hull would poke out past the surface as a dark crescent beside it.
- **The collider is still what you see.** Every animal starts from its lab prop's collider and stays inside it, as the other soft props do. A feature that has to stand out of it (ears, a head, horns) is laid out in `level.ts` and built by the physics as drawn: `BUNNY` ears as rounded boxes in `pieceBoxes`, the giraffe's head, muzzle, ears and horns as balls in `pieceBalls`, the snake's head from `snakeHead`, the eel's tail fins from `eelFins`. All of it switches on `softProps()`, so the lab look keeps its own colliders. Nothing is ever drawn with no collider under it, and nothing solid is invisible.
- **Each animal has its own colours.** Obstacles were white with one accent; animals are the exception and carry a body colour of their own, kept pastel so they sit in the blue world, plus one or two feature colours (pink ears, lemon fins). They are grouped per animal in `ANIMALS` so a theme can recolour one without touching the rest.
- **The accent still says what a piece does.** A piece with a mechanic keeps its light: lemon for a fixed thrust (kicker, jump pad), orange for what the ball can push or move, red for what is dangerous. On an animal that light becomes a feature in that colour (a button, a crest, a nose), never a strip.
- **Pushable animals wear a green dome.** A small green half-ball (`ANIMALS.push`) hovers over the pig, cow, owl, chick and sliding turtle, so a player knows at a glance what moves. It hovers like the jump pad's rings and is not solid.
- **Orientation stays free.** Platforms can turn, tilt, roll and twist, and the floor texture gives none of that away (round patches, tufts turned every which way). Animals ride their piece's frame, so a rolled pillar is a giraffe lying down, which is fine.

Bridges and gates stay as they were (apart from the gate's cube, which is the chick): they are structure the animals stand on and hang from. Fences, rails and hoops share one rail, so they share one animal: the snake's body is drawn by `snakeBody` in rails.ts and its head by `snakeHeadMesh`, and the three only differ in where the head goes (`snakeHead`, a rails end, `ringHead`). A tube's mouth rings are the same ring but belong to the eel, so they are its lips and its tail rather than snakes.

## Flat pieces are painted

The plank, board, seesaw and pangolin are panels the ball rolls over, so their animals are paint only: wings, spots, eyes and teeth as flat discs sunk into the face. The same goes for anything on a jump pad's top. A part that stood up there would cost the ball speed or catch it, and the piece's job comes first.

The pangolin bends as it unrolls, so its paint can't be fixed discs: each colour is one mesh whose vertices ride the body at their own place along it every frame (`buildPangolin` in scene.ts), so a scale bends round the coil and lies down the sloping head, and the ink outline shares that mesh.
