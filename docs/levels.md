# Controls and level format

## Controls

Aerox-style: left / right swings the camera around the ball, up / down rolls it toward or away from the camera.
Keyboard arrows or WASD, mouse drag (either button) to turn the camera and heading as you drag, touch drag as a virtual stick, or device tilt (the "Tilt to steer" switch under Options or in the pause menu; iOS asks for motion access, and the angle the device is held at when it is switched on, or when a level starts, counts as level).
In a level played from the admin panel or the editor: `T` or the Tune button opens the feel-tuning panel (gravity, throttle, climb assist, speed cap, damping, tilt range, the camera's pitch, bumper, kicker and magnet strength, and the gravity scale shared by movable props: crates, bridge planks and knock-down planks; its Reset leaves the player's own settings alone), `R` respawns (and so does falling 8 below the level's lowest platform, ramp end, path node, moving-platform stop or prop: `respawnY` in level.ts; a crate or barrel that falls that far goes back to where it started), `Esc` or the Menu button pauses and opens the in-game menu (Resume, Restart, Settings, Quit; Settings shows the player's settings with Back to the menu). The player's settings, there and under Options on the home screen: turn speed, mouse sensitivity, camera distance (3 to 7, default 4), camera height (1 to 4, default 2) and tilt to steer.
Climb assist (`climbAssist`, 0.4 by default) cancels that share of gravity's pull down a slope while the ball is pushed up it.
Tuning values persist in the browser; the panel's Copy button gives them as JSON to paste into `src/tuning.ts`.

## Levels

Each level is a JSON file in `src/levels/`, picked up automatically and listed in file-name order. An optional `"thumb": {"x", "y", "z", "r", "yaw"?}` sets what its menu card pictures: that point, framed with about `r` round it, the camera turned `yaw` degrees round it from the usual angle; without it the card shows the level's most telling piece. A level is a list of pieces:

| type      | fields                                   | notes |
|-----------|------------------------------------------|-------|
| `start`   | x y z                                    | one per level: a low round nest (radius 1.03, rim 0.22 high) whose centre is carved into a bowl 0.75 across and 0.16 deep; the ball spawns resting in the bowl |
| `goal`    | x y z                                    | one per level: a disc of radius 1 (fixed: any `r` in the file is ignored) with a beam of light 20 units tall; touching the beam anywhere, airborne included, ends the level |
| `slab`    | x y z w d rot tilt roll? twist? move? | flat platform, top surface at y; `tilt` (degrees about the local x axis, pivoting on the centre) leans it, 90 stands it up as a wall; `roll` (degrees about the local z axis) leans it the other way, applied before `tilt`; `twist` (degrees) rolls it along its length instead: flat at its near end (local +z) so it joins the platform before it, turning evenly to `twist` at its far end (local -z) about the centre line of its top. A twisted slab stays part of the welded rolling floor, its fences twist with it (without the light strip), and it carries no holes. A slab with `move` {speed wait offset loop stops[{x y z wait}]} is a moving platform that travels a schedule: (x, y, z) is its start; `stops` are the other places it goes, relative to the start before `rot`. It waits `wait` seconds at the start and each stop's own `wait` there (a new moving platform, and each stop the editor adds, waits 0), and travels between them at up to `speed`, easing in and out of every stop. `loop` is `pingpong` (out to the last stop and back the same way) or `loop` (last stop straight back to the start, round again); `offset` starts it that many seconds into its schedule, to stagger several. It is solid and carries whatever rides it; the editor draws an outline at each stop and the route between them. Everything standing on a moving platform's top where the level places it (blocks, blockades, pillars, barriers, crates, stools, kickers, jump pads, hoops, planks and seesaws) rides with it, solid where it is drawn; other platforms, paths, the start and the goal never ride. A moving platform is left out of the welded floor and carries no holes. Older levels' `mover` pieces load as moving slabs; the editor's moving platform button places one, and a slab's panel has Make it move / Stop moving |

| `fence`   | x y z rot smooth? path[{x y z bend mid?}] | a single rail at fence height, placed anywhere (see [Fences](#fences)). (x, y, z) is its first node and `path` the rest, relative to it before `rot`, laid out exactly like a `tube`'s; a node's y is the surface it stands on |

(x, y, z) is end a and the path's last node end b. An end's y is simply the top of the platform it attaches to (a whole number: 2 for a platform at y 2), and the editor snaps each end onto its platform like a side plank; the attachment height is added for you. An in-between node's y is the height the ball rolls at. Wherever a rail meets a platform it goes in square, never on a slope. So rails run from a side end on a higher platform down to a top end on a lower one. Two rails carry the ball round a curve when it is steered along them at a sensible speed; at full speed off a platform edge it flies past the start of a curve, and on one rail a curve at speed throws it off |
| `plank`   | x y z w h rot tilt side? freeze?         | knock-down plank: a panel `w` wide, `h` tall and 0.12 thick standing straight up on the surface at y, on a hinge through the middle of its base at (x, z), running along local x and held 0.07 above the surface in a yoke at each side. It is a free body like a crate with its base pinned: it starts at `tilt` degrees about its hinge (0 upright, positive leaning toward local -z, 90 or -90 lying flat) and is live under physics from the start, so a leaning plank falls on its own. With `freeze` (the editor's "freeze until touched", off by default) it holds still at its start angle until anything moving (the ball, a crate, another plank) touches it, then follows the push over, in either direction, and lies flat as a crossing; it can stand mid-platform too. The exception is a **side plank** (`side: true`): it hangs on the platform's side wall instead of standing on top. Put (x, z) on the edge line at the platform's top `y`, with local +z pointing into the platform; the hinge sits 0.10 outside the wall and 0.34 below the top, in a white bracket with two green bands bolted to the wall at each side, and the panel stands up past the top. Its collider is exactly the panel, `w` × `h` × 0.12; the hinge barrel (no thicker than the panel) and the brackets are drawn only, and in the editor its selection outline is the panel alone. | `seesaw`  | x y z w d rot tilt freeze?               | a board `w` wide, `d` long (along local z) and 0.1 thick, pinned at its middle on an axle 1.2 above the surface at y between two solid posts. With `freeze` it holds its start angle, solid but unmoved, until something touches it. The tilt is limited to where an end rests on the surface, about 16° for the default 4 by 8 |
| `support` | x y z w h rot                            | three pillars standing against a platform's side wall and carrying a platform `h` layers above (4 by default). It is drawn in the kicker's exact white, dark tread, pale slats and orange. The four ramp faces are alike, each with a dark slatted vent over most of it, and each corner has an orange arc over the middle half of its curve; in the middle of the top is the launch square, 0.3 of the pad's smaller side across (1.2 on the default), with three faint amber squares hovering over it up to 0.5 above the top, each a little narrower than the one below, breathing in and out on x and z a beat behind the one below so they ripple up like a pyramid (drawn only; the launch column stays the launch square). Node y is the tube's inner floor, so a mouth set on a platform top is flush with it and the ball rolls straight in. The path is straight segments between nodes; each in-between node's `bend` is the radius of the arc that rounds that corner (default 1.5), or 0 for a sharp mitred elbow. A node's optional `mid` {x y z} (relative, like the nodes) curves the segment arriving at it: seen from above the segment follows the circle through its start, `mid` and end, and its height changes smoothly along the whole curve through the three heights, so a curve that climbs climbs all the way round. A corner next to a curved segment is not rounded. With `smooth: true` the path is instead one smooth curve through every node with no corners at all (each node's heading points from the node before it to the node after; `bend` and `mid` are ignored); on rails it leaves and meets each end's stub along the stub's heading. New rails start smooth; the path panel's shape button switches a tube or rails between smooth curve and corners & arcs, and on a smooth path dragging the small dot between two nodes pulls a new node out there. Each mouth has a ring bent from the fences' rail round it (the hoop's ring), its opening exactly the bore and centred on the mouth so the tube's end sits inside it and the ring coats it, and solid. Nothing about a tube is checked: mouths, heights and turns are up to the designer. Nothing pushes the ball through: it runs either way, and inside the tube the player's push drives it along the tube, so a climb is made by holding forward against gravity and letting go slides it back. Collared mouths, both cyan |
| `hoop`    | x y z rot                                | a tube mouth's ring standing alone: an upright ring bent from the fences' and rails' own rail (the same 0.11 thick tube and metal, with the rails' light strip round its outside), its opening exactly the tube's bore (radius 0.51, a hair wider than the ball), facing along local z. It stands where a tube mouth on the surface at y would be, 0.01 higher so its opening's bottom clears the surface (the ball catches on an edge level with the floor), the rail below sunk into the floor; the ball rolled straight at it goes through, brushing the rim with only a hair to spare; solid, drawn and in the physics alike. Snaps and takes its y like a structure |

`rot` is degrees about the vertical axis.

### Rolled props

Blockades, barriers, pillars, columns, bumpers, magnets, hoops, crates and barrels take an optional `roll` (degrees): the prop turns that far about its own front-to-back axis (local z, after `rot`) through its base point (a crate's or barrel's centre), drawn and solid alike. Pillars, columns, bumpers and magnets also take an optional `rot` for which way they roll. A rolled prop keeps the `y` it is given instead of settling onto the surface, so it can stand out of a wall: a magnet with `roll` 90 on a wall face sticks straight out of it. A bumper's bounce and a magnet's pull work in the prop's own frame: a rolled bumper throws the ball out from its own axis, and a rolled magnet pulls the ball toward its own axis, so one on a wall above the ball's path pulls it up. The level part of a magnet's pull is held under `magnetHold` of the throttle (the push is level, so driving away always escapes); the upright part is only the magnet's own `magnetForce`, enough to lift the ball against gravity. A crate or barrel starts rolled and rests at its rolled height (a barrel with `roll` 90 lies on its side). In the editor these props have roll fields, and Y / Shift+Y roll them 15°. Kickers and jump pads roll their own way (see their rows); slabs roll with `tilt`.

### Fences

A fence is its own piece, not part of a platform: one rail 0.6 above the surface (`FENCE_RAIL_Y`; its centre above a resting ball's centre, so it meets the ball above its middle and a ball rolling into a fence, even at full speed, is pushed down and back and never rides over it; only a jump clears one), run along a node path exactly like a tube's or the rails', so it can span several platforms, stop anywhere and turn corners. Each node's y is the surface the fence stands on there; the editor sets it from whatever is under the node, so a fence follows steps and ramps node by node. Corners are rounded on 0.2 (`bend`, `FENCE_RAIL_CORNER`), a segment's `mid` curves it, and `smooth` makes the whole fence one curve. Both ends turn straight down on that 0.2 corner into the surface, 0.1 below it. A light strip runs along the rail's right-hand side seen walking from the first node to the last, stopping 0.25 short of each end: run a fence clockwise round a platform (seen from above) and the strip faces in. The collider is exactly the rail.

In the editor, the fence button adds a straight fence 8 long; select it to drag its nodes and use the same path panel as tubes and rails (Split, Remove node, Extend, Reverse, smooth curve or corners & arcs). A selected platform also has a "fence a side" row with one button per side (n e s w; a ramp's e w; a curve's a outer b inner) that adds a fence piece along that whole side, 0.32 in from the edge (`FENCE_RAIL_INSET`), running clockwise.

Older level files that set `fences` on a platform (per side `true`, `false` or `[from, to]` spans, clockwise from each side's start) still load: each run of fenced sides that meet at a corner becomes one fence piece. Fences on a tilted slab are dropped.

### Hitboxes

Every collider is exactly what is drawn: there are no invisible walls, and nothing drawn is left without a collider. A fence is its rail tube and nothing more. Small details (light strips, rims, panels, screens, vents, chevrons) are sunk into the surface they sit on until they stand no more than 0.005 proud, so they read as paint and the body's collider is the whole shape. Rounded blocks, crates, stools, barrier pods and seesaw posts collide with the same rounding they are drawn with; a moving or tilted slab collides as the hull of its own drawn mesh; pillars, the start pad, the goal's disc and glow ring, the spinner's hub, a seesaw's hub and axle stubs, a plank's mounts and barrel and a bridge's barrels and wall lugs are all solid as drawn. The one deliberate exception is the seam where two platforms meet: drawn as a full groove, it is only a faint dip in the physics so the ball rolls straight across (platforms.md). Press H in the editor, or the Hitboxes button, to see every collider as the physics builds it, drawn in magenta over the level; it updates a moment after each change and is remembered.

### Units

| axis | 1 unit is | notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| x, z | one placement-grid cell | new pieces are sized in multiples of 4, the standard look-right unit: a new slab is 8 wide and 16 long, a new curve 8 wide; lane width 8; in the editor platforms snap to 4 and structures and holes to 1 by default (change either with the Platform snap and Structure snap pickers); in the side panel, platform sizes and heights (slab and ramp `w` `d` `y`, curve `inner` `outer` `y`, ramp `rise`, bridge `w` `d`) step and round to 4 unless Fine sizes is ticked; ball diameter 1.1 |
| y    | one platform thickness, which is one height layer | platforms sit on whole or half numbers (5 or 5.5) and stack when one is a layer above another; the ball is 0.5 in radius, 1.0 tall (four of a platform's pixel tiles across; it just fits under a platform whose underside is one unit up, a platform two layers above another); a ramp rises `rise` layers, 4 by default; fences are 0.9 tall, blockades 1.5, pillars 2.2 |

The editor snaps platform, bridge and plank `y` to half a layer (PgUp/PgDn or E/Q step one layer, Shift+E/Q half a layer, or type 5.5 into the y field) and structures take their `y` from the platform under them. R turns the selection 90° about the vertical axis; T tilts a slab 90° about its own x axis and Y rolls it 90° about its own z axis (Y rolls a kicker or jump pad 15°) (Shift turns back; the tilt and roll fields take any angle). A tilted or rolled slab is solid on every face but is not part of the welded rolling floor, so it has no rounded lip, is skipped by the overlap check, and cannot carry holes or structures. Structures (`block`, `blockade`, `pillar`) snap to the structure grid in the editor (1 unit unless changed with Structure snap) and drop onto the platform beneath them; the grid is drawn whenever a structure is selected. The white ground grid sits at the height in the toolbar's Grid y field (whole layers, remembered in the browser), and a piece added with nothing selected is placed at that height. Slabs and curves may touch along an edge but must not overlap; the editor paints overlapping platforms red and lists them under problems, and `npm run check` rejects levels that have any. A selected tube shows a white handle at each node and a smaller mint dot halfway along each segment. Drag a node to move it; E / Q raise or lower it, arrows nudge it, B switches it between a sharp elbow and a smooth bend, Delete removes it. Drag a mint dot to bend that segment into a curve through it (it snaps to the structure grid, at most 0.5); E / Q raise or lower it half a layer, arrows nudge it, Delete or the panel's straighten button makes the segment straight again. A curve stays inside the box between its segment's two nodes: its point can't be dragged, nudged or typed past either node on any axis, and if the curve through it would still bulge out of that box the point is pulled back toward the straight segment until it fits (so a segment running straight along one axis can't curve off it). The path panel has a curve row above each node with the point's x, y and z, each showing the range it must stay in; an axis where both nodes agree is fixed. Moving a node refits the curves next to it. The panel lists the nodes with the turn at each, and Split, Extend and Reverse edit the path. Use the in-game editor to lay a level out and press Save or Ctrl+S. Ctrl+Shift+S on the menu opens the admin panel: a card per level (click the picture to play it, Edit to open it, Delete to remove its `src/levels/<id>.json` after a confirm, dev only), and a + card that starts a blank level. There are no drafts: a level exists only as its saved file, so leaving the editor without saving loses the changes, and the menu reads the level files fresh from disk each time it opens (in `npm run dev`), so Edit always starts from what was last saved. ▶ Play runs from the start pad; ▶ Play At (or P) arms a ghost ball that follows the cursor over any upward surface, and a click plays from that spot, facing the way the editor camera looks (Esc cancels): only in `npm run dev`, Save writes `src/levels/<id>.json` directly (a deployed build refuses to save), replacing the level with that id. There is no access control yet.

The editor's add palette is grouped: Platforms (slab, moving platform, curve, ramp, bridge, hole), Interactables (kickers, jump pad, planks, seesaw, stool, crate, barrel, bumper, magnet, blockade, barrier, pillar, hoop), Connectors (tube, rails, fence) and Misc (start, goal, support, column, and any piece not yet sorted). The spinner and the plain block are no longer offered, though old levels that have them still load. Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Shift+Z (or Ctrl+Y, or the Redo button) redoes what was undone; a new edit clears the redo. The round ← at the top left leaves the editor.
