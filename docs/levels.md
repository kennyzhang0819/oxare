# Controls and level format

## Controls

Aerox-style: left / right swings the camera around the ball, up / down rolls it toward or away from the camera.
Keyboard arrows or WASD, mouse drag (either button) to turn the camera and heading as you drag, touch drag as a virtual stick, or device tilt (enable it on the menu; iOS asks for permission).
In a level played from the admin panel or the editor: `T` or the Tune button opens the feel-tuning panel (gravity, throttle, speed cap, damping, tilt range, camera, and the gravity scale shared by movable props: crates, bridge planks and knock-down planks), `R` respawns, `Esc` or the Menu button pauses and opens the in-game menu (turn speed and mouse sensitivity sliders, Resume, Restart, Quit).
Tuning values persist in the browser; the panel's Copy button gives them as JSON to paste into `src/tuning.ts`.

## Levels

Each level is a JSON file in `src/levels/`, picked up automatically and listed in file-name order. A level is a list of pieces:

| type      | fields                                   | notes |
|-----------|------------------------------------------|-------|
| `start`   | x y z                                    | one per level: a low round pad (radius 1.3, height 0.22) the ball spawns on top of |
| `goal`    | x y z r                                  | one per level: a disc with a beam of light 40 units tall; touching the beam anywhere, airborne included, ends the level |
| `slab`    | x y z w d rot tilt fences{n,e,s,w}       | flat platform, top surface at y, fences on local sides before rotation, whole or partial (see [Fences](#fences)); `tilt` (degrees about the local x axis, pivoting on the centre) leans it, 90 stands it up as a wall |
| `curve`   | x y z inner outer rot fences{inner,outer,a,b} | 90° annulus around (x, z), sweeping from local +x to local -z; `a` and `b` fence the two ends; fences whole or partial (see [Fences](#fences)) |
| `ramp`    | x y z w d rot rise fences{e,w}           | level landing, S-curve climb through the middle half, level landing: from height y at local +z to y + rise layers at local -z; `rise` may be negative |
| `mover`   | x y z w d rot speed wait offset loop stops[{x y z wait}] | moving platform: a slab `w` by `d` with its top at y that travels a schedule. (x, y, z) is its start; `stops` are the other places it goes, relative to the start before `rot`. It waits `wait` seconds at the start and each stop's own `wait` there, and travels between them at up to `speed`, easing in and out of every stop. `loop` is `pingpong` (out to the last stop and back the same way) or `loop` (last stop straight back to the start, round again); `offset` starts it that many seconds into its schedule, to stagger several. It is solid and carries whatever rides it; the editor draws an outline at each stop and the route between them |
| `bridge`  | x y z w d rot                            | hanging plank bridge, `d` long along local z and `w` wide, slung between the platform edges at its two ends; `y` is the top of those platforms and the hinge line sits 0.36 below it, at the upper light line of the edge strip, so the planks meet the bottom of the platform lip. One hinged plank per cell; the planks are free bodies that sag and swing under the ball |
| `rails`   | x y z rot lines a b path[{x y z bend mid?}] | an open tube: rails the same thickness as the fence rails, laid along a node path exactly like a `tube` (straight segments, `bend` rounding a corner, `mid` curving a segment, the same editor handles and path panel). `lines` 2 is two rails 1 apart (centre to centre) with the ball riding between them; 1 is a single rail to balance on. (x, y, z) is end a and the path's last node end b. An end's y is simply the top of the platform it attaches to (a whole number: 2 for a platform at y 2), and the editor snaps each end onto its platform like a side plank; the attachment height is added for you. An in-between node's y is the height the ball rolls at. Wherever a rail meets a platform it goes in square, never on a slope. A `top` end stands over a platform top with the rail at fence-rail height (0.45) and turns straight down into it on the fence's 0.2 corner, exactly as a fence rail ends; a ball riding the rails drops off it onto the platform. A `side` end goes level and square into the middle of the nearest open platform wall (the dark line of its edge strip), 0.4 deep; a ball rolls off that platform's edge onto the rails. Next to each end the rail runs a level stub 0.9 long (at least 0.5 of it dead straight), square out of the wall for a side end or straight back from the turn down for a top end; only past the stubs do the rails slope or turn, bending into them on a radius of 1. So rails run from a side end on a higher platform down to a top end on a lower one. Two rails carry the ball round a curve when it is steered along them at a sensible speed; at full speed off a platform edge it flies past the start of a curve, and on one rail a curve at speed throws it off |
| `plank`   | x y z w h rot tilt side? freeze?         | knock-down plank: a panel `w` wide, `h` tall and 0.12 thick standing straight up on the surface at y, on a hinge through the middle of its base at (x, z), running along local x and held 0.07 above the surface in a yoke at each side. It is a free body like a crate with its base pinned: it starts at `tilt` degrees about its hinge (0 upright, positive leaning toward local -z, 90 or -90 lying flat) and is live under physics from the start, so a leaning plank falls on its own. With `freeze` (the editor's "freeze until touched", off by default) it holds still at its start angle until anything moving (the ball, a crate, another plank) touches it, then follows the push over, in either direction, and lies flat as a crossing; it can stand mid-platform too. Make `h` at least half a cell longer than the gap so the tip lands on the far platform rather than its rounded edge. **Every plank must be pushed back 0.5 tiles from the platform's border**: put its hinge 0.5 in from the edge it stands on, never on the edge itself. The exception is a **side plank** (`side: true`): it hangs on the platform's side wall instead of standing on top. Put (x, z) on the edge line at the platform's top `y`, with local +z pointing into the platform; the hinge sits 0.10 outside the wall and 0.34 below the top, in a white bracket with two green bands bolted to the wall at each side, and the panel stands up past the top. It starts anywhere from upright to hanging straight down (`tilt` 0 to 180: 90 is level, past 90 angles down below the edge), never leaning in. Its collider is exactly the panel, `w` × `h` × 0.12; the hinge barrel (no thicker than the panel) and the brackets are drawn only, and in the editor its selection outline is the panel alone. Once touched it swings freely either way with full collision and comes to rest on whatever it hits: lying flat its top meets the bottom of the platform lip, like a bridge plank, so make `h` longer than the gap and its tip rests on a far platform at the same height; over a lower platform it swings down until its tip lands there, making a slope; shorter than the gap with nothing below, it hangs straight down. Pushed toward its own platform it leans back against that platform's edge. In the editor, ticking **side** hangs the plank on the nearest open platform edge (position, height and facing), and dragging it slides it along edges and hops between them |
| `seesaw`  | x y z w d rot tilt freeze?               | a board `w` wide, `d` long (along local z) and 0.1 thick, pinned at its middle on an axle 1.2 above the surface at y between two solid posts. Same body as a plank, live under physics from the start: it begins at `tilt` degrees (positive raises the local -z end) and the ball's weight tips it. With `freeze` it holds its start angle, solid but unmoved, until something touches it. The tilt is limited to where an end rests on the surface, about 16° for the default 4 by 8 |
| `support` | x y z w h rot                            | three pillars standing against a platform's side wall and carrying a platform `h` layers above (4 by default). Put (x, z) on the lower platform's edge at its top surface `y`, with local +z pointing out past the edge: the pillars sit just outside it, spread over `w`, from below the lower platform up to the upper platform's underside at y + h - 1. Each pillar stands half a unit off the lower platform's wall and its foot bends inward into that wall; the dark slotted face looks inward. The stems are solid |
| `kicker`  | x y z w d h rot                          | small solid wedge ramp on a platform: `w` wide, rising `h` over `d` toward local -z (defaults 3, 3, 1, an 18° slope), white with a dark slatted tread and orange side strips. Snaps like a structure; keep the slope gentle so the ball rolls up rather than bouncing off the front edge |
| `jump`    | x y z w d rot rise                       | jump pad: a low white platform `w` × `d` (default 4 × 4) on the surface at y, 0.35 tall, with a 1-long ramp up to its flat top on every side (so a 2 × 2 top by default, about 19° slopes, near the kicker's 18°). It is drawn in the kicker's exact white, dark tread, pale slats and orange. The four ramp faces are alike, each with a dark slatted vent over most of it, and each corner has an orange arc over the middle half of its curve; in the middle of the top is the launch square, 0.3 of the pad's smaller side across (1.2 on the default), with three orange squares hovering over it up to 0.5 above the top. Only that orange column launches: while the ball is in it (resting, rolling over the square, or skimming over it off the ramp's crest), its upward speed is set to what carries it to `rise` layers above the pad's top (default 4, peaking within about 0.05 of that), worked out from where it is and against the current gravity and damping, so it still holds after tuning; its speed and heading over the ground are kept, so it flies on the way it was going. The platform is solid: one convex hull, so the ramps have no seams. Snaps and takes its y like a structure; put it on a flat platform; `w` and `d` must be at least 3 |
| `block`   | x y z w h d rot                          | obstacle sitting on a surface at y |
| `blockade` | x y z rot                               | fixed 3×1.5×3 panelled crate on a surface at y |
| `pillar`  | x y z                                    | fixed cylinder column, radius 0.7, height 2.2, on a surface at y |
| `barrier` | x y z rot                                | fixed 3×1.4×0.5 pod on two legs, standing on a surface at y; its collider is exactly the pod |
| `crate`   | x y z w h d rot                          | pushable box `w` × `h` × `d` (each default 1.2; an older `s` sets all three), a free body under gravity resting on the surface at y; returns home if it falls off |
| `stool`   | x y z w h d rot track offset             | tracked movement stool: a white block `w` × `h` × `d` (defaults 2 wide, 1.2 tall, 1 thick) on an invisible track `track` long (default 8) running along its local x, centred on (x, z), on the surface at y; the editor outlines the block at both ends of its slide and draws the line between, nothing shows in play. It slides along the track and nothing else, so it can only be pushed the way the green < > chevrons on its faces point, and stops at the track's ends; pushed from any other side it holds still like a wall. It starts `offset` along the track from the centre (clamped to the track). As heavy as the ball and damped, so it moves while the ball rolls into it and soon stops after. Snaps and takes its y like a structure; `track` must be at least `w` |
| `hole`    | x y z w d rot                            | cutout through every slab whose top is at y; can cross slab edges to reshape the slab (see [platforms.md](platforms.md#holes)) |
| `spinner` | x y z length speed                       | rotating bar, speed in rad/s |
| `tube`    | x y z rot path[{x y z bend mid?}]        | glass pipe just wide enough for the ball (a single glass skin of radius 0.58 against the ball's 0.55). (x, y, z) is one mouth; `path` lists the following nodes relative to it (before `rot`), the last being the other mouth. Node y is the tube's inner floor, so a mouth set on a platform top is flush with it and the ball rolls straight in. The path is straight segments between nodes; each in-between node's `bend` is the radius of the arc that rounds that corner (default 1.5), or 0 for a sharp mitred elbow. A node's optional `mid` {x y z} (relative, like the nodes) curves the segment arriving at it: seen from above the segment follows the circle through its start, `mid` and end, and its height changes smoothly along the whole curve through the three heights, so a curve that climbs climbs all the way round. A corner next to a curved segment is not rounded. The metal collar round each mouth is solid. Nothing about a tube is checked: mouths, heights and turns are up to the designer. Nothing pushes the ball through: it runs either way, and inside the tube the player's push drives it along the tube, so a climb is made by holding forward against gravity and letting go slides it back. Collared mouths, both cyan |

`rot` is degrees about the vertical axis.

### Fences

Each fence side is `true` (fenced end to end), `false` (open), or a list of `[from, to]` spans, so a fence can stop partway or leave gaps: `"n": [[0, 4], [6, 10]]` fences a 10-wide slab's north side except a 2-unit gap in the middle. Spans are measured clockwise round the platform seen from above, from the corner where each side starts:

| piece | side | runs from | units |
|-------|------|-----------|-------|
| `slab` | `n` `e` `s` `w` | west end, north end, east end, south end | 0 to `w` (n, s) or `d` (e, w) |
| `ramp` | `e` `w` | north (local -z) end, south end | 0 to `d` |
| `curve` | `a` | inner edge, out to the outer | 0 to `outer - inner` |
| `curve` | `outer` | end `a` (local +x), round to end `b` | 0 to 90 degrees |
| `curve` | `b` | outer edge, in to the inner | 0 to `outer - inner` |
| `curve` | `inner` | end `b`, round to end `a` | 0 to 90 degrees |

Spans past a side's length are clipped to it. Where two fenced spans meet at a corner the rail turns the corner in one piece; every other end of a span curves down into the platform. The collider is exactly the spans.

In the editor, select a platform: the side panel lists each side with a tick (fence it end to end, or clear it), each span's from and to, ✕ to drop a span, and split to cut a gap in the middle of the longest one. A cyan dot sits near each end of each span; drag it along its side to move that end (it snaps to the Structure snap, 5° on arcs, and stays between its neighbours).

### Units

| axis | 1 unit is | notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| x, z | one placement-grid cell | new pieces are sized in multiples of 4, the standard look-right unit: a new slab is 8 wide and 16 long, a new curve 8 wide; lane width 8; in the editor platforms snap to 4 and structures and holes to 1 by default (change either with the Platform snap and Structure snap pickers); in the side panel, platform sizes and heights (slab and ramp `w` `d` `y`, curve `inner` `outer` `y`, ramp `rise`, bridge `w` `d`) step and round to 4 unless Fine sizes is ticked; ball diameter 1.1 |
| y    | one platform thickness, which is one height layer | platforms sit on whole numbers and stack when one is a layer above another; the ball stands 1.1 tall; a ramp rises `rise` layers, 4 by default; fences are 0.9 tall, blockades 1.5, pillars 2.2 |

The editor snaps platform, bridge and plank `y` to a layer (PgUp/PgDn or E/Q step one layer, or type into the y field) and structures take their `y` from the platform under them. R turns the selection 90° about the vertical axis; T tilts a slab 90° about its own x axis. A tilted slab is solid on every face but is not part of the welded rolling floor, so it has no rounded lip, is skipped by the overlap check, and cannot carry holes or structures. Structures (`block`, `blockade`, `pillar`) snap to the structure grid in the editor (1 unit unless changed with Structure snap) and drop onto the platform beneath them; the grid is drawn whenever a structure is selected. Slabs and curves may touch along an edge but must not overlap; the editor paints overlapping platforms red and lists them under problems, and `npm run check` rejects levels that have any. A selected tube shows a white handle at each node and a smaller mint dot halfway along each segment. Drag a node to move it; E / Q raise or lower it, arrows nudge it, B switches it between a sharp elbow and a smooth bend, Delete removes it. Drag a mint dot to bend that segment into a curve through it (it snaps to the structure grid, at most 0.5); E / Q raise or lower it half a layer, arrows nudge it, Delete or the panel's straighten button makes the segment straight again. The panel lists the nodes with the turn at each, and Split, Extend and Reverse edit the path. Use the in-game editor to lay a level out and press Save or Ctrl+S. Ctrl+Shift+S on the menu opens the admin panel: a card per level (click the picture to play it, Edit to open it, Delete to remove its `src/levels/<id>.json` after a confirm, dev only), a card for the editor's unsaved draft while it differs from every saved level (playable and editable the same way; Delete discards the draft), and a + card that starts a blank level. ▶ Play runs from the start pad; ▶ Here (or P) arms a ghost ball that follows the cursor over any upward surface, and a click plays from that spot, facing the way the editor camera looks (Esc cancels): only in `npm run dev`, Save writes `src/levels/<id>.json` directly (a deployed build refuses to save and keeps the draft in the browser), replacing the level with that id. There is no access control yet.
