# Controls and level format

## Controls

Aerox-style: left / right swings the camera around the ball, up / down rolls it toward or away from the camera.
Keyboard arrows or WASD, the mouse to turn the camera and heading: with "Lock mouse to camera" on (Options or the pause menu; the default) the game captures the mouse from the moment a level starts, so it turns without end and never leaves the window (it asks for the raw mouse, without the system's pointer acceleration, and ignores any single locked movement over 250 pixels, a browser glitch that would snap the view round), Esc frees it and opens the pause menu, and closing the menu (or a click on the game) captures it again; switched off, drag with either button to turn, or touch. With "Touch slider" on (Options or the pause menu; on by default on a touch screen) a see-through slider stands on the left: slide it up to roll forward and down to roll back, and it springs back to the middle when let go; a drag anywhere else with another finger turns the camera and heading as a mouse drag does. With it off, a touch drag is a virtual stick that steers and throttles while held off centre.
In a level played from the admin panel or the editor: `T` or the Tune button opens the feel-tuning panel (gravity, throttle, climb assist, speed cap, damping, tilt range, the camera's pitch; the slide drag and slide friction that bring stools and sliding kickers to a stop soon after the ball stops pushing (they act only once the ball has been off one for 0.3 s, `SLIDE_GRACE`, so pushing it is as free as ever; they ride an exact slide joint, where a body's own damping does nothing, so the sim applies them each step); the damping that slows crates and barrels, so a pushed barrel stops rolling; its Reset leaves the player's own settings alone), `R` respawns (and so does falling 8 below the level's lowest platform, ramp end, path node, moving-platform stop or prop: `respawnY` in level.ts; a crate or barrel that falls that far goes back to where it started), `Esc` or the Menu button pauses and opens the in-game menu (Resume, Restart, Settings, Quit; Settings shows the player's settings with Back to the menu). The player's settings, there and under Options on the home screen: turn speed, mouse sensitivity, camera distance (3 to 7, default 4), camera height (1 to 4, default 2), and the switches (a custom switch, `toggle.ts`, never a native checkbox) "Lock mouse to camera", "Touch slider" and "Performance stats". With "Performance stats" on (off by default, remembered, and it takes effect at once, from the pause menu too) a readout sits in the top right while playing, under the HUD buttons, refreshed twice a second with averages over that half second: frames per second and frame time, the script time per frame and the physics steps' share of it, draw calls and triangles per frame (the whole frame: the sky pass, the ball's mirror and the main pass together, so draws alternate with the mirror's every-other-frame refresh), the drawing buffer's size and pixel ratio, and the renderer's live geometries, textures and shader programs, plus the JS heap where the browser reports it. The tune panel moves down below it.
The speed cap (`maxSpeed`, 6.9) limits only the player's own push: throttle and climb assist never take the ball's level speed (over the ground, or over a moving platform carrying it) past it, and at the cap the push still turns the ball without speeding it up, but bumpers, jump pads, slopes, magnets and props can carry it faster, and damping then slows it back. Wall grip (`wallGrip`, 1): while the ball is off the ground and pressed against the big face of a slab tilted or rolled near upright (a wall; not its edges, a platform's side, a fence or a block), that face carries up to that share of its weight, no more than the press allows (steer into the wall to press harder), so hugging a wall carries a jump further. Climb assist (`climbAssist`, 0.4 by default) cancels that share of gravity's pull down a slope while the ball is pushed up it, but only the part of the pull straight against the push: driving across a slanted platform, or up it at an angle, the ball still slides sideways down it as it would with no assist.
Tuning values persist in the browser; the panel's Copy button gives them as JSON to paste into `src/tuning.ts`. Bumper bounce and kick and the magnet's force and hold are settled: they live in `src/tuning.ts` but are not in the panel and never read back from the browser.

## Levels

Each level is a JSON file in `src/levels/`, picked up automatically and listed in file-name order. The level select has a tab per world along its top (`WORLDS` in level.ts), each listing that world's levels, numbered within it and labelled with the world's `tag` (C1, C2 in Classic, N1, N2 in Neo, W1, W2 in Workshop, the community levels); an optional `"world": "neo"` or `"world": "workshop"` puts a level in that world, and a level without one is in Classic. Every world is open to players; one listed in `LOCKED` (menu.ts) shows to them with a lock and can't be opened, while the admin panel opens every world. Everything but the levels sits in that top bar (Back, the tabs, the admin tools, and under them the Grid / List switch, remembered in the browser, and the pager), and the levels scroll below it a page at a time: 24 cards to a grid page, 50 rows to a list page, only the page shown being built. ← and → turn the page. An optional `"hidden": true` keeps the level out of the player's level list (numbered and "next" over the public ones only), for playgrounds: it still shows, dimmed, in the admin panel, whose ⋯ menu switches a level between public and hidden. A level file without it is public, but a new level starts hidden, so nothing reaches players until it is made public. "Next" after a level stays in its world. An optional `"thumb": {"x", "y", "z", "r", "yaw"?}` sets what its menu card pictures: that point, framed with about `r` round it, the camera turned `yaw` degrees round it from the usual angle; without it the card shows the level's most telling piece. A level is a list of pieces:

| type      | fields                                   | notes |
|-----------|------------------------------------------|-------|
| `start`   | x y z                                    | one per level: a low round nest (radius 1.03, rim 0.22 high) in the platforms' white, its outer top edge rounded (0.12) and a glowing cyan ring on its flat rim, whose centre is carved into a bowl 0.75 across and 0.16 deep; the ball spawns resting in the bowl. Drawn and solid alike |
| `goal`    | x y z                                    | one per level: a disc of radius 1 (fixed: any `r` in the file is ignored) with a beam of light 20 units tall; touching the beam anywhere, airborne included, ends the level |
| `slab`    | x y z w d rot tilt roll? twist? curl? move? belt? glass? shape? | flat platform, top surface at y; `shape` varies its edges (docs/platforms.md "Shaped slabs"): for each side (`n` the far side, local -z; `e` local +x; `s`; `w`) how far its two ends are pushed out (negative pulls them in), named by their corners (`e: {n, s}`, `n: {w, e}`), and `bow`, how far its middle bows out (negative in) from the straight line between its ends, all in units on the 0.5 grid, so `shape: {e: {n: -1, s: 1}, w: {n: -1, s: 1}}` on an 8-wide slab runs from 6 wide at its far end to 10 at its near end. A shaped slab is flat and still: it can't tilt, roll, twist, move or be a treadmill, and every end must stay at least 1 across; `tilt` (degrees about the local x axis, pivoting on the centre) leans it, 90 stands it up as a wall; `roll` (degrees about the local z axis) leans it the other way, applied before `tilt`; `twist` (degrees) rolls it along its length instead: flat at its near end (local +z) so it joins the platform before it, turning evenly to `twist` at its far end (local -z) about the centre line of its top. A twisted slab stays part of the welded rolling floor, its fences twist with it (without the light strip), and it carries no holes. A slab with `move` {speed wait offset loop stops[{x y z wait}]} is a moving platform that travels a schedule: (x, y, z) is its start; `stops` are the other places it goes, relative to the start before `rot`. It waits `wait` seconds at the start and each stop's own `wait` there (a new moving platform, and each stop the editor adds, waits 0), and travels between them at up to `speed`, easing in and out of every stop. `loop` is `pingpong` (out to the last stop and back the same way) or `loop` (last stop straight back to the start, round again); `offset` starts it that many seconds into its schedule, to stagger several. It is solid and carries whatever rides it; the editor draws an outline at each stop and the route between them. Everything standing on a moving platform's top where the level places it (blocks, blockades, pillars, barriers, crates, stools, kickers, jump pads, hoops, planks and seesaws) rides with it, solid where it is drawn; other platforms, paths, the start and the goal never ride. A moving platform is left out of the welded floor and carries no holes. Older levels' `mover` pieces load as moving slabs; the editor's moving platform button places one, and a slab's panel has Make it move / Stop moving. A slab with `belt: true` is a treadmill (see [Treadmills](#treadmills)). A slab with `glass: true` is a glass slab (see [Glass slabs](#glass-slabs)); one with `curl` (degrees) bends up along its length like a quarter pipe or a loop (see [Curled slabs](#curled-slabs)) |
| `curve`   | x y z inner outer rot sweep? roll? rollAt? | a turn round the corner (x, z) from end `a` (along local +x, from `inner` to `outer`) to end `b`: 90° (the default, `b` along local -z), with `sweep` 180 a C, two corners in one piece with no seam, `b` coming back level with `a` and facing local +z, or with `sweep` 270 a 3/4 ring, `b` heading local +x on the far side of `a`'s line, 4 past the corner (the editor's C curve and 3/4 curve buttons, or the curve's turn field; a 3/4 curve's `inner` must be at least 4 or its ends would cross). The editor's radius field moves `inner` and `outer` together, keeping the lane's width. Each end runs 2 dead straight (less if `inner` is under 2) before the arc, so the ends meet other pieces square; the arc turns about the point 2 in from the corner on both axes, so the ends sit exactly where a plain quarter ring's would and the arc bulges about 0.83 out at its middle; `a` and `b` fence the two ends; fences whole or partial (see [Fences](#fences)) |
| `ramp`    | x y z w d rot rise                       | level landing, S-curve climb through the middle half, level landing: from height y at local +z to y + rise layers at local -z; `rise` may be negative |
| `bridge`  | x y z w d rot                            | hanging plank bridge, `d` long along local z and `w` wide, slung between the platform edges at its two ends; `y` is the top of those platforms and the hinge line sits 0.36 below it, at the upper light line of the edge strip, so the planks meet the bottom of the platform lip. One hinged plank per cell; the planks are free bodies that sag and swing under the ball |
| `fence`   | x y z rot smooth? path[{x y z bend mid?}] | a single rail at fence height, placed anywhere (see [Fences](#fences)). (x, y, z) is its first node and `path` the rest, relative to it before `rot`, laid out exactly like a `tube`'s; a node's y is the surface it stands on |
| `rails`   | x y z rot lines a b aYaw? bYaw? smooth? path[{x y z bend mid?}] | an open tube: rails the same thickness as the fence rails, laid along a node path exactly like a `tube` (straight segments, `bend` rounding a corner, `mid` curving a segment, the same editor handles and path panel). `lines` 2 is two rails 0.92 apart (centre to centre) with the ball riding between them; 1 is a single rail to balance on. (x, y, z) is end a and the path's last node end b. An end's y is simply the top of the platform it attaches to (a whole number: 2 for a platform at y 2), and the editor snaps each end onto its platform like a side plank; the attachment height is added for you. An in-between node's y is the height the ball rolls at. Wherever a rail meets a platform it goes in square, never on a slope. A `top` end stands over a platform top with the rail at fence-rail height (0.45) and turns straight down into it on the fence's 0.2 corner, exactly as a fence rail ends; a ball riding the rails drops off it onto the platform. A `side` end goes level and square into the middle of the nearest open platform wall (the dark line of its edge strip), 0.4 deep; a ball rolls off that platform's edge onto the rails. An `end` end attaches to nothing and closes the rails: its node's y is the ball's rolling height like an in-between node's (the editor leaves it where it is), and two rails are joined there by a level half circle (one rail carries on round into the other, the light strip round the outside), while a single rail is rounded off with a ball of its own radius. The bend is at rail height, solid as drawn: it catches a ball that coasts into it, but a ball still being pushed rolls up over it. Next to each end the rail runs a level stub 0.9 long (at least 0.5 of it dead straight), square out of the wall for a side end or straight back from the turn down for a top end; only past the stubs do the rails slope or turn, bending into them on a radius of 1. `aYaw` / `bYaw` (degrees, 0 toward local -z, turning like `rot`) set the way an end's stub heads instead, for an end whose worked-out heading leaves the rails kinked; in the editor pick the end's node and press R / Shift+R to turn it 15 degrees, or type it in the path panel's turn field (auto clears it). So rails run from a side end on a higher platform down to a top end on a lower one. Two rails carry the ball round a curve when it is steered along them at a sensible speed; at full speed off a platform edge it flies past the start of a curve, and on one rail a curve at speed throws it off |
| `plank`   | x y z w h rot tilt side? freeze? base?   | knock-down plank: a panel `w` wide, `h` tall and 0.12 thick standing straight up on the surface at y, on a hinge through the middle of its base at (x, z), running along local x and held 0.07 above the surface in a yoke at each side. It is a free body like a crate with its base pinned: it starts at `tilt` degrees about its hinge (0 upright, positive leaning toward local -z, 90 or -90 lying flat) and is live under physics from the start, so a leaning plank falls on its own. With `freeze` (the editor's "freeze until touched", off by default) it holds still at its start angle until anything moving (the ball, a crate, another plank) touches it, then follows the push over, in either direction, and lies flat as a crossing; it can stand mid-platform too. Make `h` at least half a cell longer than the gap so the tip lands on the far platform rather than its rounded edge. **Every plank must be pushed back 0.5 tiles from the platform's border**: put its hinge 0.5 in from the edge it stands on, never on the edge itself. The exception is a **side plank** (`side: true`): it hangs on the platform's side wall instead of standing on top. Put (x, z) on the edge line at the platform's top `y`, with local +z pointing into the platform; the hinge sits 0.10 outside the wall and 0.34 below the top, in a white bracket with two green bands bolted to the wall at each side, and the panel stands up past the top. It starts anywhere from upright to hanging straight down (`tilt` 0 to 180: 90 is level, past 90 angles down below the edge), never leaning in. Its collider is exactly the panel, `w` × `h` × 0.12; the hinge barrel (no thicker than the panel) and the brackets are drawn only, and in the editor its selection outline is the panel alone. Once touched it swings freely either way with full collision and comes to rest on whatever it hits: lying flat its top meets the bottom of the platform lip, like a bridge plank, so make `h` longer than the gap and its tip rests on a far platform at the same height; over a lower platform it swings down until its tip lands there, making a slope; shorter than the gap with nothing below, it hangs straight down. Pushed toward its own platform it leans back against that platform's edge. In the editor, ticking **side** hangs the plank on the nearest open platform edge (position, height and facing), and dragging it slides it along edges and hops between them `base` (0 by default, the panel's base field) raises the hinge that much higher off the surface, the yokes standing taller to hold it, each then with a green band near its top and its foot; `h` is still the panel's own height. A raised plank knocked over swings past lying flat until its top end meets the surface. A side plank ignores `base` |
| `seesaw`  | x y z w d h rot tilt freeze? dips?       | a board `w` wide, `d` long (along local z) and 0.1 thick, pinned at its middle on an axle `h` above the surface at y (1.2 by default; the posts are 0.3 taller, so `h` raises the whole base) between two solid posts. Same body as a plank, live under physics from the start: it begins at `tilt` degrees (positive raises the local -z end) and the ball's weight tips it. With `freeze` it holds its start angle, solid but unmoved, until something touches it. The tilt is limited to where an end rests on the surface, about 16° for the default 4 by 8 at 1.2; a taller axle lets it tip steeper. Nothing hangs under the board, only the axle stubs at its edges: the ball can roll in under a raised end and on past the axle (at the default `h` 1.2 the board's underside clears the ball by 0.15) and push the lowered end up from beneath. With `dips` (`"+z"` or `"-z"`, the editor's dips picker) only that end of the board goes down: the axle stops it at level the other way, so weight on the other end, however much, leaves it level, and its start `tilt` is held to that side (the editor marks the dipping end with a yellow arrow) |
| `board`   | x y z w d rot tilt roll? freeze?         | a loose board, the seesaw's tiled board with its green rim, the knock-down plank's 0.12 thick, `w` across and `d` along (4 by 8, the plank's size, by default). It is turned like a slab: `roll` degrees about its own z, then `tilt` about its own x (positive raises the local -z end), then `rot`, and rests with its lowest corner on y. Under physics it only moves up and down, each end of its length on its own: it slides straight up and down and turns about the level line square to its length through its centre (square to its width if it stands on end), nothing else, so it never slides sideways, spins or topples, and pushed up under one end only that end lifts. It weighs 0.15 of a ball, so a seesaw under one end lifts it easily with the ball or a cube on its other end; a board standing on two one-way seesaws (`dips`) rests on them without tipping them, and rises fully only when both are pressed and falls under the same gravity as the ball from the start, or with `freeze` holds its place, solid but unmoved, until something touches it (so one can hang in the air and drop with the ball). It does not ride moving platforms |
| `support` | x y z w h rot roll? reach?               | three pillars standing against a platform's side wall and carrying a platform `h` layers above (4 by default). The bend curves straight into the wall, with nothing round the joint, and is solid as drawn, as is the stem. It wears the pillar's colours: white, slate, pale grey and cyan. Near each end of the straight stem a slate collar runs round it, dipping on the front and back faces with its ends turned toward that end. The stem between the collars is split into stretches about 2.6 long. Each stretch carries, front and back, a slate panel ribbed in pale grey inside a pale border between two cyan lines, and an ear out each side, 0.22 past the stem and narrowing toward its tip, with a slate pad on its front and back. The ears are solid as drawn. The stems are solid |

### Rolled props

## Treadmills

A treadmill is a slab with `belt: true`, any `w` and `d` of at least 2. Its outside is exactly a slab's: the same walls, edge strip, lips, border and rounded corners, the same seams with the platforms around it, fences and snapping. Only the tiled floor is gone, top and bottom: everything more than 0.675 in from the edge (where a slab's tiles start) is opened right through and filled with fat rods running across it (along local x), shared by both faces. Each rod is as thick as the platform less 0.04, centred in it, so it nearly reaches the top and the bottom, and the rods are spread evenly along the opening, about one every 0.95: an 8 by 8 treadmill holds 7, and a longer one gets more. The rods turn so their tops run toward the far end (local -z, the way `rot` 0 faces) and their bottoms back the other way; turn the slab with `rot` to point it. Each rod carries orange chevrons pointing the way its surface runs, lined up across the rods, so the tops show one pattern running forward and the underside the same pattern running back.

The rods are solid as drawn and really spin. A ball on them rides along at `beltSpeed` (3, in the tuning panel), and keeps that speed rolling off the far end. The ball sits a little down in the dips between the fat rods and bumps over them. Throttle pushes as on the ground, so pushing with the belt goes faster and full throttle against it climbs back up it slowly. The opening's edge drops square into the rods, with no lip. A treadmill can't tilt, roll, twist or move, and holes don't cut it. In the editor the treadmill button adds one 8 by 8, and any slab's panel has a treadmill checkbox.

Blockades, barriers, pillars, columns, bumpers, magnets, hoops, crates and barrels take an optional `roll` and `tilt` (degrees): the prop turns `roll` about its own front-to-back axis (local z) and then `tilt` about its own side-to-side axis (local x), both before `rot`, through its base point (a crate's or barrel's centre), drawn and solid alike. Pillars, columns, bumpers and magnets also take an optional `rot` for which way they roll. A rolled or tilted prop keeps the `y` it is given instead of settling onto the surface, so it can stand out of a wall: a magnet with `roll` 90 on a wall face sticks straight out of it. A bumper's bounce and a magnet's pull work in the prop's own frame: a rolled bumper throws the ball out from its own axis, and a rolled magnet pulls the ball toward its own axis, so one on a wall above the ball's path pulls it up. The level part of a magnet's pull is held under `magnetHold` of the throttle (the push is level, so driving away always escapes); the upright part is only the magnet's own `magnetForce`, enough to lift the ball against gravity. A crate or barrel starts turned and rests at its turned height (a barrel with `roll` or `tilt` 90 lies on its side, a crate tilted 90 on its front face). In the editor these props have tilt and roll fields; Y / Shift+Y roll them 15° and T / Shift+T tilt them 15°. Kickers and jump pads take `roll` and `tilt` the same way (see their rows; a sliding kicker takes neither); slabs have their own tilt and roll.

## Glass slabs

A glass slab is a slab with `glass: true`: its tiled top and underside are one see-through pane of the tubes' glass, and everything else is a slab's as before: the same white rim, lips, light strip and walls, the same seams, fences, snapping and holes. A hole cut in a glass slab keeps its rounded rim, so the pane shows a framed opening. The pane casts no shadow; the rim does. The physics never sees the glass: the ball rolls on it as on any slab. A treadmill has no top to turn to glass, so `glass` does nothing on one. In the editor the glass button adds one 8 by 16, and any slab's panel has a glass checkbox.

## Curled slabs

A curled slab is a slab with `curl` (degrees): instead of lying flat it bends up along its length, flat at its near end (local +z, the end that joins the platform before it) and turned `curl` degrees at its far end, bending evenly, so its top runs round a circle whose radius is its depth over the curl in radians: 16 deep and curled 90° it is a quarter pipe of radius 10.2, curled 180° a half loop of radius 5.1 that ends 10.2 up and upside down, and curled 360° a full loop that comes back to where it started. A negative curl bends down. The rim, light strip, lips and tiles bend with it, as does an older level's fence on it. A curl must be within ±360°, and its radius at least 2 so the ball has room inside: the editor flags a slab that is too shallow for its curl. A curled slab is in the welded floor like a twisted one (docs/platforms.md "Curled slabs"), so the ball rolls onto it from the flat slab before it without a hop; speed is all that carries it round. It can't tilt, roll, twist, move, be shaped or a treadmill, and holes don't cut it. Its footprint, for snapping and overlaps, is the part of its top before it turns vertical. In the editor the curl button adds one 8 by 16 curled 180°, and any slab's panel has a curl field (steps of 15°).

### Fences

A fence is its own piece, not part of a platform: one rail 0.6 above the surface (`FENCE_RAIL_Y`; its centre above a resting ball's centre, so it meets the ball above its middle and a ball rolling into a fence, even at full speed, is pushed down and back and never rides over it; only a jump clears one), run along a node path exactly like a tube's or the rails', so it can span several platforms, stop anywhere and turn corners. Each node's y is the surface the fence stands on there; the editor sets it from whatever is under the node, so a fence follows steps and ramps node by node. Corners are rounded on 0.2 (`bend`, `FENCE_RAIL_CORNER`), a segment's `mid` curves it, and `smooth` makes the whole fence one curve. Both ends turn straight down on that 0.2 corner into the surface, 0.1 below it. A light strip runs along the rail's right-hand side seen walking from the first node to the last, stopping 0.25 short of each end: run a fence clockwise round a platform (seen from above) and the strip faces in. The collider is exactly the rail.

In the editor, the fence button adds a straight fence 8 long; select it to drag its nodes and use the same path panel as tubes and rails (Split, Remove node, Extend, Reverse, smooth curve or corners & arcs). A selected platform also has a "fence a side" row with one button per side (n e s w; a ramp's e w; a curve's a outer b inner) that adds a fence piece along that whole side, 0.32 in from the edge (`FENCE_RAIL_INSET`), running clockwise.

Older level files that set `fences` on a platform (per side `true`, `false` or `[from, to]` spans, clockwise from each side's start) still load: each run of fenced sides that meet at a corner becomes one fence piece. Fences on a tilted slab are dropped.

### Hitboxes

Every collider is exactly what is drawn: there are no invisible walls, and nothing drawn is left without a collider. A fence is its rail tube and nothing more. Small details (light strips, rims, panels, screens, vents, chevrons) are sunk into the surface they sit on until they stand no more than 0.005 proud, so they read as paint and the body's collider is the whole shape. Rounded blocks, crates, stools, barrier pods and seesaw posts collide with the same rounding they are drawn with; a moving or tilted slab collides as the hull of its own drawn mesh; pillars, the start pad, the goal's disc and glow ring, the spinner's hub, a seesaw's axle stubs, a plank's mounts and barrel and a bridge's barrels and wall lugs are all solid as drawn. The one deliberate exception is the seam where two platforms meet: drawn as a full groove, it is only a faint dip in the physics so the ball rolls straight across (platforms.md). Press H in the editor, or the Hitboxes button, to see every collider as the physics builds it, drawn in magenta over the level; it updates a moment after each change and is remembered.

### Units

| axis | 1 unit is | notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| x, z | one placement-grid cell | new pieces are sized in multiples of 4, the standard look-right unit: a new slab is 8 wide and 16 long, a new curve 8 wide; lane width 8; in the editor platforms and structures (and holes) both snap to 2 by default (change either with the Platform snap and Structure snap pickers, remembered in the browser); in the side panel, platform sizes and heights (slab and ramp `w` `d` `y`, curve `inner` `outer` `y`, ramp `rise`, bridge `w` `d`) step and round to the platform snap; ball diameter 1.1 |
| y    | one platform thickness, which is one height layer | platforms sit on whole or half numbers (5 or 5.5) and stack when one is a layer above another; the ball is 0.5 in radius, 1.0 tall (four of a platform's pixel tiles across; it just fits under a platform whose underside is one unit up, a platform two layers above another); a ramp rises `rise` layers, 4 by default; fences are 0.9 tall, blockades 1.5, pillars 2.6 |

The editor snaps platform, bridge and plank `y` to half a layer (PgUp/PgDn or E/Q step one layer, Shift+E/Q half a layer, or type 5.5 into the y field) and structures take their `y` from the platform under them. Every piece may carry `"mirror": true`, flipping it left for right across its own length (M, or the panel's mirror box); only pieces that are not already symmetric take it (`MIRRORED` in level.ts: the side kicker, for now), and on any other piece it is refused as a problem. R turns the selection 90° about the vertical axis: one piece about its own origin, several together as one rigid group about their middle (rounded to the grid, so every piece lands back on its own grid), each moved round it and turned the same, so they keep their places to each other; E / Q raise or lower everything selected together, and with several selected the panel has a "raise by" field (any multiple of half a layer, negative lowers), ▼ / ▲ layer buttons and a ⟳ 90° button; T tilts a slab 90° about its own x axis and Y rolls a slab or curve 90° about its own z axis (Y rolls a kicker or jump pad 15°, T tilts one 15°) (Shift turns back; the tilt and roll fields take any angle). A tilted or rolled slab is solid on every face but is not part of the welded rolling floor, so it has no rounded lip, is skipped by the overlap check, and cannot carry structures. It can carry holes turned the same way (see `hole`): then it collides as the floor it would have lying level, holes, lips and all, turned into place. A curve takes `roll` too: it turns about the centre line of one end, level with its top, the way a slab rolls about its own centre line (right-handed about that end's outward heading). That end stays exactly where it was and banks off the platform it meets, while the other end swings round. Turned rigidly the swinging end would land off the grid (rolled 30 it moves by cos 30), so a rolled curve is bent very slightly to land it on the nearest whole unit across and half layer up, the nudge fading in from nothing at the end it turns about. Both ends stay square, so a C rolled 30 about end `b` at x 8 puts its end `a` at x 17, y 10, where a slab rolled 30 meets it; unrolled, the piece would not quite be a C, which is fine, since it is shaped for its roll. It is end `a` unless `"rollAt": "b"` (the panel's roll about), so roll about whichever end has to meet something exactly: a slab twisted 30 meets a curve rolled 30 about the end it touches with no gap. Rolled 180 it hangs upside down. A rolled curve is likewise out of the welded floor and the overlap check, takes no fences, structures or holes, and collides as its flat floor would (top, rounded lip, sides and underside) rolled with it. Structures (`block`, `blockade`, `pillar`) snap to the structure grid in the editor (2 units unless changed with Structure snap) and drop onto the platform beneath them; the grid is drawn whenever a structure is selected. The white ground grid sits at the height in the toolbar's Grid y field (whole layers, remembered in the browser), and a piece added with nothing selected is placed at that height. Slabs and curves may touch along an edge but must not overlap; the editor paints overlapping platforms red and lists them under problems, and `npm run check` rejects levels that have any. A selected tube shows a white handle at each node and a smaller mint dot halfway along each segment. Drag a node to move it; E / Q raise or lower it, arrows nudge it, B switches it between a sharp elbow and a smooth bend, Delete removes it. That moves the node alone, a tube's first mouth included (its row in the path panel has its own x, y and z for it); the piece's x, y and z move the whole tube, and dragging the whole tube drops its first mouth onto the platform under it, the rest following. Drag a mint dot to bend that segment into a curve through it (it snaps to the structure grid, at most 0.5); E / Q raise or lower it half a layer, arrows nudge it, Delete or the panel's straighten button makes the segment straight again. A curve stays inside the box between its segment's two nodes: its point can't be dragged, nudged or typed past either node on any axis, and if the curve through it would still bulge out of that box the point is pulled back toward the straight segment until it fits (so a segment running straight along one axis can't curve off it). The path panel has a curve row above each node with the point's x, y and z, each showing the range it must stay in; an axis where both nodes agree is fixed. Moving a node refits the curves next to it. The panel lists the nodes with the turn at each, and Split, Extend and Reverse edit the path. Use the in-game editor to lay a level out and press Save or Ctrl+S. Ctrl+Shift+S on the menu opens the admin panel (Back, or Ctrl+Shift+S again, returns to the player's list): the world tabs, + New level (a blank level in the editor), Rebuild thumbs, and a card per level (click the picture to play it, Edit to open it; under ⋯, Duplicate, Make hidden or Make public, Move to another world, and Delete to remove its `src/levels/<id>.json` after a confirm, all dev only; + New level starts the level in the world whose tab is open). Menu card pictures are saved files, `public/thumbs/<id>.png`, listed in `public/thumbs/index.json` with a key of the level's pieces and framing they were taken from: the editor's Save writes the level's picture with it, and the admin panel's Rebuild thumbs re-renders them all, which is needed only after a change to how pieces look (code, not level files). A card whose picture is missing or older than its level is rendered live instead, so it is never wrong, only slower. There are no drafts: a level exists only as its saved file, so leaving the editor without saving loses the changes, and the menu reads the level files fresh from disk each time it opens (in `npm run dev`), so Edit always starts from what was last saved. ▶ Play runs from the start pad; ▶ Play At (or P) arms a ghost ball that follows the cursor over any upward surface, and a click plays from that spot, facing the way the editor camera looks (Esc cancels): only in `npm run dev`, Save writes `src/levels/<id>.json` directly (a deployed build refuses to save), replacing the level with that id. There is no access control yet.

The editor's right sidebar holds the level's name and id at the top, then the toolbar, snap pickers and add palette; selecting pieces opens their options in a second sidebar just left of it, gone again when nothing is selected. The add palette is grouped: Platforms (slab, treadmill, curve, C curve, 3/4 curve, ramp, hole; a moving platform is a slab given a schedule in its Movement section), Interactables (bridge, kickers, jump pad, planks, seesaw, board, stool, bean, crate, barrel, bumper, magnet, blockade, barrier, gate, pillar, hoop), Connectors (tube, rails, fence) and Misc (start, goal, support, column, and any piece not yet sorted). The spinner and the plain block are no longer offered, though old levels that have them still load. Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Shift+Z (or Ctrl+Y, or the Redo button) redoes what was undone; a new edit clears the redo. The round ← at the top left leaves the editor.
